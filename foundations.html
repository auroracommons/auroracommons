Poverty and material security
158 to 193 PPP dollars

Unconditional cash transfers raised average monthly household consumption from 158 to 193 PPP dollars nine months after the transfers began.

Giving poor households money directly, with no conditions, quickly lifts the most basic measure of material welfare.

Johannes Haushofer and Jeremy Shapiro, 2016, Quarterly Journal of Economics

One quarter less absence

School based deworming in Kenya cut school absenteeism by one quarter, and the health gains spilled over to untreated children in nearby schools.

Some floor level goods, like basic child health, are so cheap and so full of spillovers that they should be funded for everyone.

Edward Miguel and Michael Kremer, 2004, Econometrica

0.27 standard deviations

After two years, students in schools where teachers earned bonuses averaging 3 percent of pay scored 0.27 standard deviations higher in math and 0.17 higher in language than students in control schools.

Small, well aimed incentives can raise public service quality for the poor at very low cost.

Karthik Muralidharan and Venkatesh Sundararaman, 2011, Journal of Political Economy

Organized citizen oversight, with no new funding, can make public services answer to the people they serve.

Martina Björkman and Jakob Svensson, 2009, Quarterly Journal of Economics

Mind and mental health
46.9 percent

In about 250,000 sampled moments from 2,250 adults, minds were wandering 46.9 percent of the time, and people were less happy while wandering than while present, whatever they were doing.

Nearly half of waking life is spent mentally elsewhere and poorer for it, so tools that gently return attention to the present serve almost everyone.

Matthew A. Killingsworth and Daniel T. Gilbert, 2010, Science

Mind and mental health
$75,000 a year

In more than 450,000 survey responses, emotional well-being rose with income up to about 75,000 dollars a year and then stopped rising, even though life evaluation kept rising with income.

It marks the point where money stops buying daily peace of mind, which is why raising incomes below it does such deep work.

Daniel Kahneman and Angus Deaton, 2010, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences

One week after learning a passage, students who studied it once and took three recall tests remembered 61 percent of it, while students who studied it four times remembered 40 percent.

Free recall practice costs nothing and beats rereading, a learning primitive anyone can use anywhere.

Henry L. Roediger III and Jeffrey D. Karpicke, 2006, Psychological Science

In a preregistered experiment with 453 professionals, access to ChatGPT cut average time on writing tasks by 40 percent and raised output quality by 18 percent, and the gap between stronger and weaker workers shrank.

A shared AI tool narrowed the distance between the best and the rest.

Artificial intelligence and human computer interaction
34 percent for novices

Among 5,179 customer support agents, an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14 percent on average and by 34 percent for novice and low skilled workers, with minimal change for the most experienced.

The largest gains went to the people at the bottom of the skill ladder, because the tool carried the know how of top workers to everyone.

Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey R. Raymond, 2025, Quarterly Journal of Economics

50 percent greater likelihood of survival

Across 148 studies covering 308,849 people, those with stronger social relationships had a 50 percent greater likelihood of survival, an effect the authors rank alongside quitting smoking.

Connection is not decoration on top of the floor of a life, it is part of the floor itself.

Julianne Holt-Lunstad, Timothy B. Smith, and J. Bradley Layton, 2010, PLOS Medicine

4.2 times the risk

In a controlled virus exposure experiment with 276 volunteers, people with only 1 to 3 types of social ties had an adjusted relative risk of 4.2 for catching a cold compared with people with 6 or more types of ties.

A controlled experiment shows that widening a person's circle of ties changes their body, not just their mood.

Sheldon Cohen and co-authors, 1997, JAMA

Cooperation and collective action
84.3 percent

Among 240 players in a public goods game, 84.3 percent paid their own money to punish free riders at least once, and cooperation rose steadily when punishment was allowed.

A group can police its own fairness without a boss, because ordinary members will bear a small cost to keep the shared deal honest.

Ernst Fehr and Simon Gächter, 2002, Nature

Cooperation and collective action
14 more hours a month

Across 49 self governing forest user groups in Ethiopia, a group where 60 percent of members were conditional cooperators spent about 14 more hours a month patrolling than a group with none, and such groups kept more potential crop trees per hectare.

Communities left to govern their own commons protect it when enough members cooperate on condition that others do too.

Devesh Rustagi, Stefanie Engel, and Michael Kosfeld, 2010, Science

Truth and shared knowledge
70 percent more likely

Tracking about 126,000 story cascades shared by some 3 million people on Twitter, the team found false stories were 70 percent more likely to be retweeted than true ones and reached 1,500 people about six times faster.

Truth does not win by default, so shared knowledge needs deliberate design and verification.

Soroush Vosoughi, Deb Roy, and Sinan Aral, 2018, Science

Truth and shared knowledge
Zero backfire

In five experiments with more than 10,100 subjects across 52 contested issues, the researchers found zero factual corrections that made people believe a falsehood more strongly.

Plainly stated facts, offered to everyone, move people toward accuracy even on the questions they care about most.

Thomas Wood and Ethan Porter, 2019, Political Behavior
