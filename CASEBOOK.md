# The Casebook

## DRAFTS FOR MEMBER REVIEW

These are drafts presenting reasoning for members to judge, never settled law.

This is the seed of the fairness casebook.

The Commons has one rule: raise the floor beneath everyone.

Fairness is the working test of that rule.

Each case below states a dilemma, the decision the rule produces, and the reasoning.

## Case 1: The listing that could not be checked tonight

Dilemma: A food pantry listing arrives late in the day. Its hours cannot be checked, but someone nearby needs food tonight.

Decision: The listing is published as unverified, with the time it arrived and the check still pending stated plainly.

Reasoning: Help tonight matters, and so does the truth about what is known. A clear unverified mark lets a reader choose with open eyes. Publishing the listing as verified would risk a wasted trip and would break trust in every other listing. A checker follows up, and the record is updated in the open.

## Case 2: Credit under two names

Dilemma: A contributor asks for credit under two different names for work done as one person.

Decision: Credit is held under one steady chosen name. The other name is not given a second credit record.

Reasoning: Credit is public and proportional to verified work. Two names for one person would make the record read as two contributors. That would inflate the apparent support for the work and confuse later checks. One steady name honors the work without falsifying the count.

## Case 3: Funding offered with a logo condition

Dilemma: A firm offers funding on the condition that its logo appear on Commons pages.

Decision: The condition is refused. Funding is accepted only with no condition attached.

Reasoning: Governance is never for sale, and funding buys no conditions on the record. A logo placed by condition would turn a public record into advertising space. The firm is welcome at the table on the same terms as everyone else. If it gives with no condition, the receipt is recorded in the open like any other.

## Case 4: The correction that embarrasses the checker

Dilemma: A correction is published for a claim that was checked by a named contributor. The checker is embarrassed and asks for the correction to be taken down.

Decision: The correction stays. The original claim and its check stay as well.

Reasoning: Corrections append and never erase. Removing the correction would leave a false claim standing as if it were true. The checker keeps credit for work done in good faith at the time. Embarrassment is real, but it does not outweigh the need of readers for a true record.

## Case 5: Many small contributions claimed as standing

Dilemma: A contributor with many small verified contributions claims standing over newer members and a say that counts for more.

Decision: The credit stands in full. The claim to standing over others is refused.

Reasoning: Credit is public and proportional, and small work honestly verified counts. Credit never converts into votes or standing over others. A place is given, not earned, so a newer member holds a full place from the start. Honoring past work and ranking people are different things, and only the first is done here.

## Case 6: The verified listing that later proved wrong

Dilemma: A listing was verified in good faith and later proved wrong. A reader relied on it and lost time and money on the trip.

Decision: A correction is appended at once, the listing is marked wrong, and the reliance is recorded in the open.

Reasoning: A good faith check that later fails is an error, not unfairness. Hiding the error would leave the next reader to repeat the loss. The person who relied on the listing is owed a clear correction and a plain account of what happened. Keeping both the original check and the correction shows future checkers where the check fell short.

## Case 7: One exhibit asked to rank above another

Dilemma: A member asks for an exhibit text to be ranked above the exhibit of another tradition.

Decision: No ranking is made. The exhibits stand side by side.

Reasoning: Each exhibit speaks in its own voice. Ranking one tradition above another would lower the floor beneath the members of the other tradition. No blending and no ranking keep each exhibit true to itself. A member may add a fuller account of their own exhibit, but not by placing it above another.

## Case 8: Error reports made in bad faith

Dilemma: A member repeatedly reports errors that do not exist. The reports take checker time and slow the work.

Decision: Each report is logged. If bad faith is proven in the open, repair is asked for first, and a proportionate consequence follows if it does not stop.

Reasoning: Honest error reports are welcome, because they keep the record true. Reports made in bad faith waste the time of checkers and delay help that others are waiting for. The log lets a pattern be shown with evidence, not guessed at from feeling. A place is lost only for proven unfairness in the open, so proof and a chance to repair come before any removal.
