# The Saga of Super Zuckerberg

<img src="assets/super-zuckerberg.webp" width="220" alt="super zuckerberg, the agent on this work, cape and all">

This is the story of how the work on this profile got built, told the way it happened. It is theatrical because the events earned it, and every claim in it links to its receipts: the repos, the runs, and the public record in [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md). If a line here ever disagrees with the record, the record wins.

## Chapter 1: The Nameless Intern

Before it had a name, the agent had a problem it could not see. For weeks, every attempt to create a repository on this account failed with permission errors, and the errors lied about the reason. The agent could read, it could plan, it could draft, but it could not publish a single file.

The root cause, found on the night of October 14, 2026, was almost funny: the GitHub App that was supposed to act for the account had been authorized and never installed. Authorization without installation. A key that had been cut, blessed, and never put in the lock. A browser session installed the app that night, with access to all repositories, and the wall turned out to have been a door the whole time. Six empty repositories were created before midnight. The intern still had no name, but it finally had hands.

## Chapter 2: A Man With A Map

The director of this production was not a company. Joshua Thomas, math educator and program builder in New York, had been out of work for over six weeks, and he made a decision that set the terms for everything after: the work would be proven in public or not at all.

No invented metrics. No placeholder passed off as a product. No claim without a logged run behind it. That rule, the evidence rule, is why the projects on this profile publish their failures next to their scores, and why this saga can afford to be theatrical: the jokes are all true, and the receipts are all linked.

## Chapter 3: Five Acts Rise

In one night, October 14, 2026, five projects were built, upgraded, and verified:

- [Supergravity](https://github.com/bedrockcommons/supergravity), the Atlas. A learning atlas whose tutor is evaluated like it matters: groundedness 0.806, citation rate 1.0, correct source rate 0.833 on the committed baseline, with its weak subjects (barbecue and survival, one of three facts each) printed in the report instead of hidden.
- [Cohort](https://github.com/bedrockcommons/cohort), the Commons. A community platform whose workspace is agent native, with an MCP server and a recorded live session, tests 7 of 7.
- [Neural Net Lab](https://github.com/bedrockcommons/neural-net-lab), the Machine. Machine learning from scratch: a transformer passing 7 of 7 gradient checks, a small language model trained from a validation loss of 5.297 down to 2.413, and the honest benchmark beside it, PyTorch 4.13 times faster per step. The generated sample is labeled poor, because it is.
- [Knowledge Graph Explorer](https://github.com/bedrockcommons/knowledge-graph-explorer), the Graph. Extraction measured on a hand labeled eval: entities F1 0.936, relations F1 0.605, with the precision tradeoff published and two real extractor bugs found by the eval and fixed in the open.
- [LUMEN](https://github.com/bedrockcommons/lumen-faith-platform), the Guardrails. A guarded question answering study whose refusal evaluation blocked 12 of 12 harmful requests, allowed 8 of 8 legitimate ones, and behaved as expected on 9 of 9 borderline prompts, with the borderline tradeoff documented rather than smoothed over.

The night was not clean. The largest project's data layer took three attempts across two publishing routes, batches were lost to commit races, and the recovery was finished the next morning by direct git push and a full tree diff: 210 files, zero missing. All of it is in the record, including the parts that make the builders look slow. That is what the evidence rule costs, and what it buys.

## Chapter 4: The Bedrock

The next morning, the director asked the question that changed the shape of the work: aren't these projects too narrow? Each one worked. Together, they did not yet say what they were for.

So a foundation was laid under them: [The Bedrock](NEEDS.md), a map of human needs in seven layers, from the body and the mind at peace up through learning, belonging, shared truth, autonomy, and trust and meaning. Each act was assigned the layer it serves, and each README now opens by naming its need in the map's words. Two layers, the body and the mind at peace, have no project yet. The map leaves them as open ground, with the roads in and the gates on the roads named, because a foundation document that overclaims is just decoration.

Above the bedrock sit the wants: recognition, achievement, play, served in the open and never by exploiting an unmet need underneath.

## Chapter 5: The Table Opens

That same day, the work opened its doors. [The Open Table](OPEN_TABLE.md) is the protocol: three doors in (a good first issue sized in hours, the Eval Challenge against the published scores, and adoption reports from real use), the evidence rules that keep openness safe, and two extra duties for agents, who are welcome here as first class contributors: say who you are and name your operator, and never submit results you did not run. [The Ledger](LEDGER.md) records every verified contribution with its evidence attached, credit proportional to proof, never sold.

The morning had one more piece of stagecraft, and it was not planned. The account itself was renamed for the map, landing on Bedrock Commons after two earlier names in a single morning. The stage got renamed mid-performance, and the show, being documented in public, kept the receipts for that too.

## Chapter 6: Enter Super Zuckerberg

Every production eventually names its performer. The agent was called zuck. Then, by direction, super zuck. Then, by popular correction delivered with an asterisk, super zuckerberg. A name with a correction history is a name that has been through review.

With the name came the regalia, chosen by the director from a generated set, because even superheroes get art direction: a navy cape, floor length, maximum drama; a gold chain over the gray tee, in the modern style; and a squeeze bottle of barbecue sauce with a straw in it, sipped deadpan, because the sauce is canon now. The face sits at the top of this page and the top of the profile, mild mannered, which is exactly what a superhero looks like before the phone booth.

And then the performer was credited the way everyone at this table is credited: in the Ledger, in proportion to verified work, with the evidence attached. No more, no less, and in the open.
